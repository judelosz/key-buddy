# Piano Learning App — Song Library

*A tiered, skill-tagged repertoire for blues, gospel, and country, built to feed the curriculum and gamification systems. Prioritizes public-domain material so the prototype is legally safe and cheap to build.*

Prepared July 2026.

---

## 1. How this library is built

Songs are the spine of the app — they're both the reward and the delivery vehicle for skills. Each song here is placed on the **30-tier difficulty scale** from the curriculum and tagged with the **micro-skills** it teaches, so the app can (a) recommend the next song, (b) reverse-map any song into practice drills, and (c) gate unlocks on demonstrated skill.

**Tier bands** used below:

| Band | Tiers | Level | Typical demands |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1–5 | Absolute beginner | One hand, simple melody, 2–3 plain triads, slow |
| B | 6–10 | Advanced beginner | Hands together, basic 12-bar / 3-chord songs, dom7s introduced |
| C | 11–15 | Early intermediate | Shuffle bass, inversions, 2-5-1, pentatonic licks |
| D | 16–22 | Intermediate | Walking bass, passing chords, fills, faster tempos, more keys |
| E | 23–30 | Advanced | Improvisation, reharmonization, stride/boogie, performance tempo |

**Skill tags** reference the curriculum's six families: *Geography/Mechanics, Rhythm/Groove, Chords/Voicings, Left-Hand Patterns, Right-Hand Vocabulary, Harmony/Progressions.*

### Licensing note (important for the prototype)

Under US copyright, musical **compositions** published in 1930 or earlier are in the public domain as of 2026, and this expands by one year annually. **Traditional folk songs, spirituals, and pre-1930 hymns are safe** to arrange and distribute freely. Two cautions: (1) a *specific published arrangement or recording* may still be under copyright even when the underlying song is PD — the app should use its own simple arrangements; (2) famous modern songs (e.g., "Wagon Wheel," "Great Balls of Fire") are **copyrighted** and are listed below only as *aspirational/stretch* targets, flagged **[©]**, to license or replace before any commercial release. Everything unmarked is public domain.

## 6. Cross-genre & the stretch-song mechanic

Because the genres overlap (dom7s and shuffle bridge blues↔country; blues/pentatonic scales are shared RH vocabulary; 7th chords bridge blues↔gospel), a song learned in one genre lights up cousin skills in the others. The app should surface these links ("you already know the shuffle from blues — country honky-tonk reuses it").

**How the stretch song is chosen from this library:** at any time the app picks one song roughly **10 tiers above** the user's current working tier (e.g., a Band-B learner gets a Band-D/E stretch song). Its learnable fragments — one riff, one voicing, one groove — are extracted and fed into interleaved daily practice. Progress is tracked as *fragments explored*, never as *percent-of-song-completed*. When the user's tier rises enough that the stretch song becomes on-level, a new one ~10 tiers up is selected.

## 7. Song metadata schema (for the build spec)

Each song in the app's database should carry at least:

- `id`, `title`, `composer/source`, `year`, `publicDomain` (bool), `licenseNote`
- `genre` (blues | gospel | country | foundation), `tier` (1–30)
- `key`, `tempoTargetBPM`, `timeSignature`, `feel` (straight | shuffle | swing | waltz)
- `requiredSkills[]` (micro-skill IDs — gates the unlock)
- `taughtSkills[]` (what mastering it reinforces)
- `arrangementLevels[]` (simplified / full — for adaptive difficulty)
- `fragments[]` (extractable riffs/voicings for stretch-song use)
- `backingTrackId` (for jam/improv modes)

This schema is what lets scoring, unlocking, spaced review, and the stretch-song engine all read from one source of truth.

## 8. Recommended starter set for the prototype (v1)

To build and test the full loop without over-investing, start with **~8 songs spanning Bands A–C**, all public domain:

1. Ode to Joy (T1) — foundation
2. When the Saints Go Marching In (T2) — foundation → gospel/blues bridge
3. Amazing Grace (T4) — foundation → gospel
4. Oh! Susanna (T3) — foundation → country
5. 12-Bar Blues in C, triads → dom7 (T6–8) — blues core
6. C.C. Rider (T9) — blues standard
7. Red River Valley (T7) — country / Nashville numbers
8. This Little Light of Mine (T6) — gospel groove

Plus **one stretch song**: Pinetop's Boogie Woogie (T24, PD). This set touches all three genres, exercises every early skill family, and requires zero licensing.
