# Key-Buddy — Pedagogy Research Findings

*A research pass to ground the curriculum work before authoring Tiers 6+. Companion to `06-comprehensive-curriculum-plan.md` (doc-06); every finding is mapped to the doc-06 (or doc-01/doc-03) section it touches, marked **SUPPORTS**, **CHALLENGES**, or **SUGGESTS REFINING**. Produced 2026-07-28 by four parallel research passes: method-book sequencing, genre pedagogy, music cognition & motor learning, and the gamified-app landscape.*

**How to read this doc.** It is a findings inventory, not a decision log. Nothing here changes doc-06 by itself — §6 collects the concrete Tier 6–10 implications and §7 the questions for the follow-on syllabus audit, where decisions get made. Confidence grades: **high** = peer-reviewed/meta-analytic or strong multi-source convergence; **medium** = forum/practitioner consensus or few studies; **low** = single source or contested. Where the literature is thin, §8 says so instead of manufacturing certainty.

**The headline in one paragraph.** The syllabus's spine is externally validated: blues-first after I–IV–V (exactly what Alfred's adult Book 1 does), multi-component tier gates (RCM/ABRSM never gate on repertoire alone), the multi-day song-mastery floors (sleep-consolidation research), timing-as-first-class scoring (the market leader's most documented failure is ignoring rhythm), and the two-lock model (mental practice's measured effect, r ≈ 0.13, is nowhere near substitutable for physical practice). The genuine gaps found: **no damper pedal in the first half of the syllabus**, **no gospel walk-ups/walk-downs**, **no country slip-note vocabulary**, **no harmonize-a-heard-melody skill**, **lead sheets ~10 tiers too late**, **no continuity/no-stop evidence in scoring** (the thing teacher grades actually key on), and **swing grading needs a ratio-band model, not a triplet grid**. The most actionable engine-level findings: the tap-window widening fixed variance when the real problem is per-player bias; interleaving should exempt first exposures; tempo practice should alternate slow/fast rather than staircase.

## 1. Method-book & external syllabus calibration

What Faber Adult Piano Adventures, Alfred's Adult All-in-One, and the RCM/ABRSM early grades say about sequencing — where they agree (load-bearing), disagree (design freedom), and diverge from doc-06.

1.1 **Adult methods are chords-first.** Alfred's adult Level One introduces C and G7 chords at p. 20 of ~96 — before eighth notes (p. 45) or the C major scale (p. 62). Harmonic accompaniment precedes almost all rhythmic/technical vocabulary. ([Alfred TOC](https://vdoc.pub/documents/alfreds-basic-adult-piano-course-lesson-book-level-one-73mrvlebgjj0)) — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 §6 Tiers 4–5 and doc-01 §2.1 (adults leverage theory).

1.2 **Faber APA Book 1 = doc-06 Arc I.** Its structure is exactly fundamentals → I–IV–V7 in C → same chords in G; "same progression, new key" is the whole harmonic curriculum of an adult's first book. ([Faber APA 1](https://pianoadventures.com/product/adult-piano-adventures-all-in-one-course-book-1/)) — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 Tiers 4–5 and the §4.1 transposition spiral at 7–10.

1.3 **Full one-octave scales come remarkably late everywhere.** Alfred: ~65% through Book 1. Faber: Book 2 entirely; five-finger positions and pentascales (C, F, G, A, D) carry the first months. — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 §4.1's scale table; doc-06 is already slightly *earlier* than Faber — don't move scales earlier still.

1.4 **Hands-together enters immediately, but as parallel/simple-role coordination — independence comes much later.** Alfred has both hands on the grand staff by p. 12; RCM Preparatory still examines pentascales hands-*separately*; ABRSM demands hands-together scales only at Grade 1. — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 Tier 2 (LH as timekeeper) and the §4.4 technique ladder staging (true role separation at 11–15).

1.5 **The V7 *sound* arrives in month one of every adult method** (as a 3-note hand shape, years of curriculum-time before construction theory); doc-06 has no dom7 contact until Tier 7. — high. **SUGGESTS REFINING** doc-06 Tiers 4–7: seed a simplified V7 shape/sound in the Tier 4–5 I–IV–V work ("the 'wants to go home' chord"), letting Tier 7 be the construction/ear tier for an already-familiar sound.

1.6 **3/4 meter inside the first quarter of both adult Book 1s.** — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 §4.4 meter ladder and Tier 5 Amazing Grace.

1.7 **Alfred teaches syncopation at p. 46 — before scales or keys.** Single-method signal. — high for Alfred, weak as consensus. **SUGGESTS REFINING** doc-06 §4.4 (syncopation formally 11–15): a *felt* syncopation preview (ear-ID + echo-tap, not performance-gated) inside Tiers 6–10 matches both Alfred and the genres. Cheap experiment, not a mandate.

1.8 **Alfred puts a blues unit in the adult FIRST book, immediately after the primary chords in C.** The most widely used adult method treats 12-bar blues as the first application of I–IV–V. — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 Arc II Tier 6 and doc-01 §5.1. *This is strong external validation of the app's single most identity-defining sequencing decision.*

1.9 **Minor keys: Alfred ends Book 1 with A/D minor incl. i–iv–V7; doc-06 defers furthest** (minor triads T11, minor keys 15–20) — a deliberate genre trade (minor *pentatonic* at 8–12 supplies the minor sound blues uses). — high. **SUGGESTS REFINING** (mildly): keep the deferral, but make sure major-vs-minor triad *quality discrimination* (ear) lands at 6–10 as §4.4 already implies — don't let minor-quality hearing slip to Tier 11 with the triad-building skill.

1.10 **Lead sheets appear at Faber Book 2 (≈ tiers 8–13 on a 30-tier mapping); doc-06 first mentions them at Tier 21/27.** For a chord-symbol-primary app this is doubly odd — the user reads chord symbols from Tier 5. — medium-high. **CHALLENGES** doc-06 Tiers 16–27: a first "play from a simple lead sheet" (known melody + known chords) belongs around Tiers 10–13; reserve 21/27 for form markers, slash chords, first-look reading.

1.11 **Basic damper pedal is a Book-1 skill in Alfred (p. 37) and Book-2 in Faber; doc-06 has NO pedal before Tiers 23–30.** Gospel is a pedaled idiom (doc-01 §5.2's pedal-point textures). MIDI CC64 is observable, so it's honestly scorable. — high. **CHALLENGES** doc-06 §4.4/§6: add a pedal skill card around Tiers 8–12. *The one genuine first-half gap (not just tuning) the method-book pass found.*

1.12 **External difficulty calibration:** doc-06 Tier 5 ≈ RCM Preparatory / ABRSM Initial; Tier 10 ≈ ABRSM Grade 1–2 technical level with above-grade harmonic/ear literacy (~2 grades ahead). — high facts, medium mapping. **SUPPORTS** the gates, with a note for doc-06 §12: the harmonic-ahead-of-technical skew is intentional (genre app, not conservatory) — record it so future authors don't "correct" Tiers 11–22 toward classical technical difficulty.

1.13 **Both adult methods finish the trunk (reading + I–IV–V7 in 2–3 keys) in one book ≈ 6–9 months at ~30 min/day** — matching doc-06 Tiers 1–10 in scope; the walkPath perfect-player floor (<60 days to Tier 6) leaves the right headroom. — medium. **SUPPORTS** arc pacing.

1.14 **Attrition clusters around ~18 months; dropouts are the students who "achieved set goals less often," and adult quitting is shame/perfectionism-driven.** (Studies are mostly children/teens in lessons; adult-in-app is extrapolation.) — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** the zero-guilt wrap and §5.6 remediation; **SUGGESTS REFINING** Arc II–III attention: at realistic pacing the cliff lands in Tiers 8–15 — watch the Tier 10 boss-gate difficulty spike in playtesting the way the Tier-1 boss was watched.

1.15 **Where methods DISAGREE — reading approach — is exactly where Key-Buddy has freedom.** Alfred position-based, Faber guide-note/intervallic, Hal Leonard reading-centric; the only cross-method invariant is *some* staff literacy accumulating from Book 1. — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 §8.2's chord-symbols-primary + drip-fed staff stance.

1.16 **Nothing external calibrates Tiers 16–30.** Both adult courses end around triad inversions, 6/8, 16ths, multiple keys; everything in Arcs IV–V exists only in genre/jazz supplemental literature. — high. **SUPPORTS** Arcs IV–V as necessarily original design; flags that their difficulty steps will need playtest-driven tuning more than Tiers 1–15.

1.17 **RCM/ABRSM bundle ear + rhythm + reading + repertoire at EVERY level from the first exam.** No external framework gates on repertoire alone. — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 §4.5 cross-strand gates and §5.4 — the strongest point of agreement across all external frameworks.

## 2. Genre pedagogy — how blues, gospel, and country piano are actually taught

2.1 **Gospel piano is transmitted through aural apprenticeship** (recordings, church performance, mentors); commercial gospel education (Hear and Play, Apostolic Praise) is built entirely ear-first. — high. **SUPPORTS** the ear strand as first-class and §8.2's notation-secondary stance.

2.2 **"Sound before symbol" is the formalized version** (Gordon MLT, Kodály): LISTEN → SING → AUDIATE → READ → WRITE; notation as "a tool for remembering music you already understand." — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 §3.2 (Discover/Copy precede Recognize). **SUGGESTS REFINING** §8.2: staff reps should always *follow* the sounding version of the same pattern within a module, never introduce material notation-first. (The SING step has no home in a mic-less app — see §8.)

2.3 **Controlled research: ear-playing practice improves broader musicianship — as one skill in a matrix, not ear-only** (Baker & Green; McPherson's five interlocking skills). — high. **SUPPORTS** the five-strand structure and the hybrid reading position against both the pure-Synthesia and pure-notation poles.

2.4 **Lucy Green's informal-learning research:** popular musicians learn by copying recordings of music they love, working whole real songs, integrating playing/listening/improvising. — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 §2 (songs supply meaning) and the stretch mechanic. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §3.2: the "Apply" (real-song) lesson is load-bearing — modules should never ship without it; and Free Play deserves framing as pedagogy (whole-song, learner-chosen practice), not just leisure.

2.5 **Jazz-education scholarship documents the institutionalization failure mode:** when an aural tradition is formalized, theory/notation scaffolding crowds out the listening/imitation core. — medium-high. **SUGGESTS REFINING** doc-06 §12: the app IS an institutionalization of three aural traditions. Concrete guard: every genre module ships ≥1 *recording-anchored* lesson (hear the real sound, then copy) — authentic reference recordings aren't currently a first-class content entity.

2.6 **Transcription ("copying records") is phrase-level vocabulary acquisition** — imitate → transpose → recombine → personal voice; how blues players historically learned. — medium. **SUGGESTS REFINING** doc-06 §8.1: add a **"lift a lick from audio"** exercise archetype (hear a 2-bar phrase, no falling notes/notation, reconstruct it, then transpose). The single most authentic exercise in all three traditions; maps onto the Tier 11–15 call-and-response ladder.

2.7 **Form-first with plain triads is exactly how teachers stage the 12-bar** (form → dom7 color → shuffle → riffs → turnaround → improv; PianoGroove/PWJ/FreeJazzLessons all converge). — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-06 Tiers 6–7 exactly as written.

2.8 **"Internalized form" means *hearing* the change coming, not counting bars** — counting is a temporary recovery scaffold; heavy backing-track listening converts counting into hearing. — medium-high. **SUGGESTS REFINING** the Tier 6 gate: add an ear item — *predict/identify the next change from audio* — so the gate tests hearing the form, not executing a memorized map; reword §4.4's "maintain 12-bar count" toward "track the form by ear."

2.9 **Shuffle feel is taught by imitation and vocalization, never by reading swung notation.** Teachers notate straight, mark "swing feel," teach via "LONG-short"/triplet vocalization and straight→swung morphing; "swing grows out of relaxation, not speed." — high (complete convergence). **SUPPORTS** Tier 8's paired ear+hands targets. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §8: Tier 8 needs an A/B straight↔swung comparison exercise and an echo-the-feel exercise *before* any swung chart is graded; charts notate straight + `feel` marking (matching real-world convention).

2.10 **Swing-ratio research: the ratio is tempo-dependent (~2:1 average, shrinking when fast, growing when slow) and fluctuates naturally even in experts; quantized-precise long-short was rated as swinging *more*.** ([Sci Reports/PMC6934603](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934603/)) — high. **SUGGESTS REFINING** Tier 8 scoring: grade the offbeat against a **tolerance band around a tempo-appropriate ratio** (~1.7:1–2.5:1 at slow-medium tempi), not a fixed triplet grid — and report a measured "your swing ratio," an honest un-gameable feel metric consistent with guardrail #8.

2.11 **A "swing dial" is a proven-in-classroom, app-shaped teaching device** (Ethan Hein: cycle a track through swing percentages so students hear the effect directly). — medium. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §8.3: add a swing-dial ear exercise (same cell at 50/58/66% swing — "which swings more / match the reference"). Trivially implementable on the Tone.js Transport.

2.12 **Documented beginner shuffle failure modes are MIDI-detectable:** (1) stiff over-voiced triplets, (2) eighths evening out toward straight as tempo rises or attention shifts, (3) accenting everything, (4) tension-rigidity. — medium. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §10 feedback + §5.6: dedicated feedback lines ("your long-short is flattening after bar 8") and remediation paths keyed to measured ratio drift.

2.13 **The canonical first shuffle bass is root-5 ↔ root-6** (the 6th is what makes it sound like blues), and teachers precede it with a deliberately *simpler* LH (quarter-note comping) so attention can go to the new thing; hands-separate → together universally. — high. **SUPPORTS** the macro-ordering (comping T6 → shuffle T8 → walking T16 → boogie T24). **SUGGESTS REFINING** Tier 8's movement spec: "LH long-short root/**fifth**" should be authored as **5↔6 motion**, not a static root-fifth.

2.14 **Improvisation enters on day one in genre teaching, via call-and-response with a tiny vocabulary** (one motif or five pentatonic notes; mandatory space; blues form framed as conversation). — high pattern / medium placement. **SUPPORTS** Tier 8's 2-bar-answer gate. Mildly **CHALLENGES** overall placement: the tradition puts micro-improvisation (vary the echo, choose your ending note) inside Arc I — §4.4's repertoire ladder Tiers 1–2 already names "choose a starting note or ending"; make sure authored content actually ships those choice moments.

2.15 **Gospel's entry skill is "harmonize a heard melody"** (GospelKeys: scale → map chords to scale tones → harmonize any melody → common bass → hymns by pattern). Doc-06 has **no harmonize-a-heard-melody skill anywhere**. — medium-high. **CHALLENGES** the gospel band: add it around Tiers 11–15 (diatonic triad qualities are already at T13): hear a familiar melody → find it → attach I/ii/IV/V/vi under each note. Also the single best Head→Hands bridge exercise the ear strand could ask for.

2.16 **Walk-ups/walk-downs are named, early, load-bearing gospel vocabulary** (the classic 1→4 walk-up drilled in all 12 keys as foundational; easier than extensions or passing chords — stepwise diatonic bass). Currently absent from doc-06. — high. **CHALLENGES** Arc IV: add a walk-up/walk-down skill at ~Tier 11 — it pairs with the T11 Saints gospel arrangement and previews T16 walking bass (§3.5's own "shuffle becomes walking bass" spiral logic misses that walk-ups are the gospel stepping stone).

2.17 **Gospel extensions-vs-passing-chords ordering has no consensus** (PWJ inverts doc-06's T18/T19 order); the only practitioner invariant is "after dom7 fluency." — low-medium. **SUGGESTS REFINING** (weakly): treat as swappable at authoring time; content-author note, not a restructure.

2.18 **The Floyd Cramer slip-note is *the* signature country piano technique and is entirely missing from doc-06.** A whole-step-below note slipping into the chord tone (2→3), pentatonic-derived, on the beat, as loud as its target — the sound of Nashville session piano. — high. **CHALLENGES** Tiers 11–15: without it the country branch never sounds like country RH. Add a slip-note skill ~Tier 13–14 (it's just 2→3 inside the major pentatonic already placed at 11–15). *Engineering note:* charts have no ornament concept; slip-notes need either explicit short on-beat NoteEvents (works today) or an ornament annotation with widened windows — the timing windows would otherwise punish the deliberately "crushed" spacing. Prototype before authoring.

2.19 **Nashville numbers are an ear tool learned through use** (chart real songs by number while listening; play in any key), not a conversion quiz. — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** T10/T13/T20 placement and the T13 "play from numbers" gate. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §8.3: the strongest NNS exercise is **hear a progression → select its numbers** (audio→numbers), pinned explicitly from Tier 10 on.

## 3. Music cognition & motor learning

The full agent report (with complete source lists) is preserved in the project history; findings here are condensed but complete.

3.1 **Music-specific interleaving evidence is real but thin** — the most-cited music study (Carter & Grahn 2016) used 10 *advanced* clarinetists with mostly non-significant results; no published music study shows interleaving beats blocking for absolute beginners. — high (that the evidence is thin). **SUGGESTS REFINING** §12/doc-01 §2.3: restate honestly — interleaving is well-supported for adult motor transfer *generally*; the music-specific evidence is directional and comes from experienced players. Keep interleaving; drop any "2× retention" claim.

3.2 **Contextual interference does help delayed transfer in adults** (meta-analysis, 34 studies: SMD 0.55; adults 0.54, but applied settings 0.37 n.s., and the novice/expert moderator *could not be tested*). — high. **SUPPORTS** §3.5 and the SessionBuilder for an adult user; **CHALLENGES** any claim the effect is established for novices.

3.3 **Novices need a solution strategy before interference pays off** — blocked practice wins acquisition for novices; moderate initial blocking then progressive randomization is the recommended compromise. — medium. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §3.2 *and the SessionBuilder code*: `interleaveRepair` should **exempt adjacent segments that are the first exposure of the same new skill** (Discover→Copy pairs may run blocked). A concrete code-level change.

3.4 **Learners misjudge blocked practice as superior — shown on piano melodies specifically** (fixed-order practice produced an *illusory* metacognitive advantage). — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** "the session builder does it, not the user." **SUGGESTS REFINING** §10: add honest copy — "mixing feels worse and works better" — so a harder-feeling session isn't read as bad pacing. No current copy says this.

3.5 **The guidance hypothesis: high-frequency concurrent feedback produces the worst retention/transfer**; systematically *reducing* feedback frequency enhances learning. — high (long-replicated). **SUGGESTS REFINING** §3.3/§3.4: falling notes, live per-note coloring, and the verdict pill are all concurrent augmented feedback. The mode ladder currently fades them at *mode boundaries* (all-on → all-off); the evidence says fade by **frequency** — e.g. alternate reps with falling notes on/off *inside* a Supported lesson. A cheaper, better-evidenced knob than tempo.

3.6 **Immediate-vs-delayed feedback is genuinely unresolved for novices.** — low (contradictory literature). The 2026-07-23 narrowing of the live flash to signal-only grades is *coincidentally* aligned with reduced-frequency feedback — worth recording in that ADR; treat further changes as an in-app A/B, not a doc decision.

3.7 **Auditory/musical concurrent feedback avoids the dependency cost that visual overlays carry** (sonification study: no withdrawal decrement). — medium. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §3.3: encode an asymmetry — **fade visual assists first**; auditory assists (metronome, hearing your notes) are far less dependency-forming. The `Assist[]` model treats them alike for the mastery star (fine); the *pedagogical fade order* shouldn't.

3.8 **Sound-before-sign has experimental support, and the active ingredient is *production/improvisation*, not recognition** (Azzara 1993: audiation-based improvisation beat controls even on notated etudes; MLT reviews consistent). — medium (mostly child/classroom). **SUPPORTS** §3.2/§8.3 ordering. **SUGGESTS REFINING** the exercise catalog: Tier 1–5 ear content is almost entirely recognition (choice prompts); the evidence points at **echo-play and improvise-an-answer** generators — §8.1's `echo play`/`fill-the-gap` exist on paper but are not implemented.

3.9 **Tap-window widening likely fixed the wrong quantity.** Untrained adults' tapping *variability* is ~20–50 ms (≈4% of interval) — two orders inside the new ±315 ms Good window. What's actually large and individual is the **systematic bias**: negative mean asynchrony (up to ~100 ms early, highly individual) plus device latency. — high descriptive stats. **SUGGESTS REFINING** the `TAP_WINDOW_SCALE=1.75` decision: **estimate and subtract each player's mean asynchrony** (running bias correction from the count-in and first bar), then keep windows near doc-03 values — a ±315 ms Good can't distinguish a player who improves from one who doesn't, which contradicts guardrail #8. (Measurable from existing attempt data before committing: mean and SD separately.)

3.10 **Timing skill and motor skill dissociate** — tempo-set and ordering manipulations moved motor variability but **not** timing variability; low temporal variability during practice facilitated transfer. — medium (piano-specific). **SUGGESTS REFINING** §3.5/§4.4: keep variation for pitch/movement/harmony cards; give **rhythm cards more consistent, stable-tempo repetition** than the interleave policy would otherwise allow.

3.11 **Slow practice doesn't automatically transfer to fast; alternating tempi beat gradual ramping on piano scales** (slow renditions can be a *different* motor solution). — medium. **CHALLENGES** §3.4 step 1 and §5.6's +4–6 BPM staircase as the default: within a lesson, prefer **paired reps — one slow, one at target** (even if scrappy) over a monotonic ladder. `AdaptationState.tempoPct` already exists; it needs an alternating variant, not a new dimension.

3.12 **Overnight consolidation is real and music-specific** (accuracy improves only across sleep; continues a second night; learning a *similar* sequence right after recall can block the gain). — high (replicated). **SUPPORTS** §5.2's ≥5-days floor with a mechanism — the strongest empirical justification for the app's hardest gate. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §3.5/builder ordering: don't schedule a highly similar variation immediately after a card's first successful rep in the same sitting. Also licenses true wrap copy: *"you'll be better at this tomorrow without touching the keys."*

3.13 **Optimal spacing gap ≈ 10–20% of the desired retention interval** (Cepeda). — high. **SUPPORTS** FSRS; **SUGGESTS REFINING** §5.2/§5.4: the 48h/72h floors correspond to a ~2–4-week retention target — state that principle next to the constants so future tuning has a rationale; if the intended horizon is "still plays it in 6 months," the floors are conservative by design, not by evidence.

3.14 **Retrieval practice is the flagship desirable difficulty**, operationalized for music in jazz pedagogy. — high. **SUPPORTS** §3.5's varied retrieval modes. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §8 priority: the highest-value retrieval mode for a *song* is **playing with the chart hidden** — exactly the `'memory'` transfer kind the app typed but never emits. Promote a "memory mode" (chart hidden after bar N) ahead of other Phase-6 candidates; right now the app has no retrieval-strength practice mode for repertoire at all.

3.15 **Error management beats error avoidance for adaptive transfer**; recommended feedback includes metacognitive self-diagnosis prompts. — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** §3.4's "failure informative." **SUGGESTS REFINING** §10: alongside the one prescriptive tip, add a self-diagnosis disclosure ("Which hand felt late to you?" → reveal the measured answer) — the "Explain my answer" idiom, extended to chart takes.

3.16 **Retention is predicted by *how* people practice, not how much** (Duke et al. 2009: no relationship with time or trials; top pianists located, isolated, and corrected errors precisely). — high. **SUPPORTS** guardrail #5 and §10's ban on equating app-time with musicianship. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §4's strands: *practice craft / self-diagnosis* — the strongest single predictor in the piano literature — is not a strand, ladder, or gate. Either add a sixth strand (metacognition) or attach it to the technique ladder at Tier 15+ with real gate evidence (e.g. the player picks which bar to drill *before* the app suggests one, scored against `barAccuracies`).

3.17 **The ~85% rule:** optimal training accuracy for a broad class of learners is ~85%; too easy and too hard both slow learning. — medium. **SUPPORTS** §5.6's 70–85% band, with the target at the *top*. **SUGGESTS REFINING**: step-up should require >85% twice **and** an at-tempo take, so the engine doesn't walk the learner off the optimum on a single easy pass.

3.18 **Adult/older-adult acquisition is largely age-robust; age costs land on *consolidation*** (more nights, not more reps, is the age-appropriate lever); 12-month RCT in older adults shows fine-motor gains concentrated in the first 6 months. — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** `assessment.repeatedSessions` (distinct-day evidence) as the correct mechanism; rep-count thresholds should stay flat. Gives doc-06 §1 an honest expectation curve.

3.19 **Bimanual independence fights a hardwired mirror bias; every specific "timeline" in the wild is blog content, not research.** — high for the bias, low for timelines. **SUPPORTS** §3.4 hands-separate-first and the Tier 2 mirror-friendly LH design; doc-06 should not adopt a timeline. The genuinely hard step is the first **non-aligned** LH pattern — which is where shuffle (Tier 8) lands.

3.20 **SDT/dropout evidence: competence and relatedness correlate with staying; autonomy was the *least-satisfied* need; "theory/solfège lessons" is a named dropout driver.** (Population: supervised children, mean age 12.9 — extrapolation.) — low-medium for transfer. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §7.0: the funnel maximizes competence but removes choice aggressively. Two cheap autonomy affordances that don't reopen decision paralysis: **choose between two offered next segments**, and **pick which weak section to drill** (which is also the metacognitive skill from 3.16). Supports theory-woven-into-playing.

3.21 **Mental practice works, modestly (meta-analytic r ≈ 0.131)** — strongest with auditory imagery driving anticipation; imagine+sing+play beat physical practice alone. — high for the small effect. **SUPPORTS** AFK existing *and* guardrail #7 empirically (nowhere near substitutable). **SUGGESTS REFINING** §7.3's mix: it's all recognition; the best-evidenced AFK activity is **auditory-imagery production** ("audiate the phrase you'll play next").

3.22 **Variable practice improves transfer in pianists specifically** (four-interval training group beat fixed-practice at novel-interval transfer). — medium. **SUPPORTS** the "Vary" node and §3.5's tier re-entry ("new key, register, tempo, feel" as the load-bearing form of resurfacing). Sharpens a known stub: chart lessons repeating *unchanged* in the 70–85% band (`variationIdx` only re-rolls generated exercises) is the **most consequential stub for transfer** — prefer same-progression-new-key over same-key repetition.

## 4. The gamified-app landscape — what works, what fails

4.1 **Simply Piano passes players who play the wrong rhythm entirely** (note-hit-only scoring, duration ignored, binary green/red; teachers report transfer students "learned absolutely nothing about rhythm and hand independence"). — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-03 §3 and doc-06 §2 ("timing is a first-class outcome") — the market leader's most documented failure is Key-Buddy's core bet.

4.2 **Flowkey's Wait Mode can't distinguish a good performance from a bad one** (no pulse requirement at all). — high. **SUPPORTS** doc-03 guardrails 5–6: wait-style scaffolds are fine *as assists*; Flowkey shows what happens when the assist IS the assessment.

4.3 **Playground Sessions grades onset-at-tempo but ignores duration and dynamics** ("play terribly and get a perfect score so long as you're pressing keys at the correct tempo"). — medium-high. **SUGGESTS REFINING** doc-03 §3.1: duration/release scoring is marked "later tiers" with no tier named — commit it to a band (e.g. alongside Tier 11–15 phrasing) so it doesn't drift indefinitely; the identical criticism will apply to Key-Buddy until then.

4.4 **Yousician: accurate per-note scoring + one-dimensional gates still produces "learning a video game" plateaus.** — medium. **SUPPORTS** §4.5 cross-strand gates and §5.5 ("no single metric can pass a tier") — the proof case.

4.5 **Duolingo Music: excellent path UX bolted onto a curriculum that can't reach the instrument** (treble-only, no LH, touchscreen chords, glacial pacing). — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** the Duolingo-*shaped* path with un-Duolingo content decisions (real MIDI, LH at Tier 2, chords at Tier 4) — the distinction is validated, not aesthetic.

4.6 **Melodics — the closest analog — shows timing-first scoring works** (early/late color grading praised as the category's most instructive feedback; anecdotal groove transfer) **but only as a practice tool** (no theory/reading/repertoire depth) **and it's steep for true beginners.** — medium. **SUPPORTS** doc-03 §3.2. **SUGGESTS REFINING**: timing-first apps systematically underestimate beginner motor windows — expect TAP_WINDOW-style tuning at *every* new exercise type (see 3.9 for the better-grounded fix).

4.7 **"Note chasing" is the teachers' consensus failure mode of falling-note UIs** (screen-matching without reading; expert sight-readers look 6–7 notes ahead — a skill falling notes structurally bypass). — medium-high. **SUPPORTS** falling-notes-as-Assist blocking the mastery star. **SUGGESTS REFINING** §8.2: enforce "tiny staff reps every tier" via content validation (does every tier actually author a notation rep?) — the note-chasing gravity is constant because falling notes are the app's primary surface.

4.8 **Automatic note-error scoring correlates with teacher grades at r ≈ 0.86** (n=29 beginners). — high finding, low-medium generalization. **SUPPORTS** doc-03 §3's foundational premise at beginner level.

4.9 **…but the divergence cases are about *continuity*, not note counts.** The same study: a burst of replayed notes scored terribly but got a B ("a single mistake"); near-perfect notes with two mid-piece *pauses* got a C. Teachers weight pulse-survival and recovery above error tallies. — high (primary source). **SUGGESTS REFINING** doc-03 §3.4 and doc-06 §4.4: add a **continuity/no-stop evidence bit** on Attempt (pulse never broken > N beats; recovered within a beat), feeding Tier 6+ gates. Doc-06 already says "keep the pulse, complete the form, recover" — the shipped ScoringEngine can't see it. Also retroactively validates the count-in early-note-buffering fix.

4.10 **Controlled efficacy evidence for ALL these apps is thin and supplement-only** (the one sizable study tested apps as supplements to human lessons; Duolingo's research measures engagement, not learning). — medium; the gap is the finding. **SUPPORTS** guardrail #8 (the timing graph as the only defensible progress claim) — and a marketing-honesty note: no app in this space can legitimately claim proven efficacy; neither should Key-Buddy's copy.

4.11 **Instant playable win in onboarding is the category's proven retention engine** (Simply Piano: playing a recognizable melody within minutes, before any theory). — medium. **SUPPORTS** §7.0 (onboarding ends inside the first lesson) and dropping calibration from onboarding — every step before the first playable note is friction the market leader proves you shouldn't have.

4.12 **Short guilt-free sessions sustain habit; streak anxiety backfires** (Duolingo compulsion criticism). — medium. **SUPPORTS** the open-ended-session ADR, zero-guilt wrap, and deferring streaks from the UI.

4.13 **"Engagement as byproduct" is the documented industry default** (Duolingo's own framing; Simply Piano's "illusion of progress," RH-melody focus neglecting LH). — medium. **SUPPORTS** doc-03 §1 and the whole guardrail architecture — this is what the guardrails exist to resist.

4.14 **A single recommended next step beats a content buffet** (Pianote students stall when content scatters across parallel tracks; Simply Piano's optional-theory dual path draws the same critique). — medium. **SUPPORTS** §7.0's one dominant action. Specific risk flagged: the **Tier 5→6 genre-branch moment must not become a buffet** — "your path continues into blues (recommended)" with the others as previews, never three equal doors.

4.15 **Uncalibrated latency producing false "you're late" judgments is a recurring category failure, and users don't self-serve calibration.** — medium. **SUPPORTS** doc-03 §3.5's concern (confirmed in the wild). **SUGGESTS REFINING** the calibration-in-Settings-only decision: the mitigations (drag tip at mean ≥60 ms, onboarding tip line) are the right shape but unverified — add a specific manual-test line: *fresh profile, deliberately mis-latent device, count sessions until the user finds calibration.* (See also 3.9: a running bias correction would make this class of failure largely self-healing.)

4.16 **Structure-before-songs can win on skill-building** (Skoove's differentiator) even with weaker gamification. — medium. **SUPPORTS** §3.2's module anatomy.

## 6. Implications for Tiers 6–10 (the next content to author)

Merged from all four passes — the working brief for the blues-branch authoring:

1. **Author Tier 6 with confidence — the form-first, plain-triads 12-bar entry is externally validated twice over** (Alfred's adult book does I–IV–V → blues in Book 1; practitioner consensus stages form before color). Add the two upgrades: an **ear item in the gate** (predict/identify the next change from audio — internalized = *heard*, not counted) and a **V7 sound preview** lesson so Tier 7's construction lands on a familiar sound.
2. **Tier 7 (dom7s): blocked-then-interleaved.** Each new chord (C7, F7, G7) gets consecutive Discover→Copy segments before the change-drill interleaves them across the four retrieval modes. Requires the `interleaveRepair` first-exposure exemption.
3. **Tier 8 (shuffle) is the hardest authoring problem in the band — sequence it: A/B straight-vs-swung identification → swing-dial comparison → echo a heard shuffle cell → play the chart** (notated straight, `feel: 'shuffle'`). The LH cell is **root-5↔root-6**. Grade against a **tempo-dependent ratio band with per-player bias correction**, not a triplet grid — a 1.75×-widened symmetric window around a swung eighth overlaps the straight position at moderate tempi, so a player who never swings could pass. Practice as **paired slow/target reps**, not a tempo staircase. Surface a measured "your swing ratio" and its drift (flattening-toward-straight is the #1 documented failure mode).
4. **Protect the Tier 8 call-and-response gate and constrain it the way teachers do:** ≤5 allowed notes or one motif, mandatory space, resolve to a chord tone. Score "in the set, in time, resolved" — never melodic creativity. And ship the smaller choice moments (pick your ending note) earlier, inside Arc I content, per the tradition.
5. **Make Tier 6–10 the anti-note-chasing band.** A repeating 12-bar form is maximally screen-memorizable: author number-prompt retrieval mid-form ("what chord is bar 5?"), at least one falling-notes-off non-checkpoint lesson per module, and a **continuity evidence item** at the Tier 10 gate (chart-hidden or name-the-bar-you're-on) so the boss means "holds the form," not "tracked the scroll."
6. **Add the pedal skill card in this band** (G1) — both methods teach basic pedal here, gospel content needs it by Arc IV, and CC64 makes it honestly scorable.
7. **Ship a listening diet.** Reference-recording listen lessons (PD-era shuffle blues piano — e.g. Pinetop Smith 1928 — is usable) in Discover lessons and the future AFK mix, so ear targets train against authentic timbres, not only the sampler.
8. **Revisits in this band are transposition-shaped and sleep-spaced:** same progression C→F→G over same-key repetition; ≥5 nights for the Tier 10 boss; no near-identical variation immediately after a first success in a sitting.
9. **Treat Tiers 8–10 as the retention danger zone** (the ~18-month dropout cliff at realistic pacing, and exactly where competitor users plateau). Keep the step-down/remediation path prominent; apply the Tier-1-boss lesson *proactively* to the Tier 10 gate in its test window — but **don't soften the ear/theory legs** of that gate; those are what every surveyed app lacks and where the moat is.
10. **Plan the Tier 5→6 branch moment as one dominant door.** "Your path continues into blues (recommended)"; country/gospel as labeled previews. Never three equal doors (the Pianote stall).

## 8. Honest gaps and thin literature

- **Novice music interleaving is essentially unstudied.** Every music CI study used advanced players; the transfer meta-analysis couldn't test the novice moderator. The app's core scheduling commitment is an extrapolation from adult lab motor tasks — resolvable only by in-app measurement, not more searching.
- **Feedback timing for novices is contradictory** (guidance hypothesis vs mental-model support). The live-verdict pill sits on this fault line; A/B it, don't doc it.
- **Swung (non-isochronous) synchronization is barely studied** — SMS research is overwhelmingly isochronous tapping. Tier 8's grading will be designed on rhythm-game convention plus the ratio studies, not direct evidence.
- **No evidence anywhere that app mastery gates transfer to away-from-app playing** — for any app, including this one. "Can the player play for a friend on a random Tuesday" has never been measured; the only real test is Jude.
- **Adult-in-app dropout dynamics are extrapolated** from supervised children/teens (whose strongest predictor — parental involvement — has no app analogue).
- **Gospel apprenticeship specifics rest on an abstract** (the best academic source 403'd) plus commercial-course philosophy.
- **Hand-independence timelines have no research basis** — every specific number found was blog content.
- **Alfred page numbers carry ±10% edition drift** (standalone vs All-in-One); ordering claims are safe, absolute-position claims approximate.
- **Melodics' groove-transfer stories are anecdotes**, partly from its own testimonial page.
- **Hal Leonard's adult sequencing resisted extraction** — "three-method agreement" framings above are really two methods plus exam syllabi.
